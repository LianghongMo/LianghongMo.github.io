About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in physics at Princeton University working on reinforcement learning. My research asks how agents can learn the structure they need — representations, goals, and credit assignment — directly from experience, in long-horizon problems with partial observation and competing objectives, rather than from fully specified rewards.

Currently I am working on scaling contrastive (goal-conditioned) RL, and developing diversity-aware credit assignment for RL fine-tuning of diffusion models, which improves both reward and sample diversity over DDPO. I also build sequence models with long memory for physical dynamics, such as a Mamba correction branch on a frozen GraphCast that cuts ten-day forecast error by 16%.

Before turning to machine learning I worked on quantum many-body dynamics — driven conformal field theories, non-Hermitian systems, entanglement structure — with seven preprints, five as first or co-first author. That background is where my interest in long-horizon control under partial observation comes from.

Diversity-Aware Step Weighting for RL Fine-Tuning of Diffusion Models

Apr. 2026 – present · code

RL fine-tuning of a pretrained generative model against a learned reward collapses diversity and invites reward hacking. This is not an optimizer bug: it is the fixed point of the reward objective, and KL regularization tilts it but does not remove it, so the reward–diversity Pareto frontier is the right target. DASW replaces DDPO's uniform per-step weight in the policy-gradient loss with a budget-conserving weight toward the denoising steps where generations still branch, measured by the feature-space spread of predicted clean images, with no diversity reward or occupancy penalty.

Result: against DDPO and reward-variance, scheduler-only, and CoCA-style baselines (Stable Diffusion v1.4 + LoRA, 3 seeds), DASW-Inception raises both LAION aesthetic reward and ImageReward while holding diversity (Vendi score), with less reward hacking; on prompt–image alignment, DASW variants occupy stronger Pareto-efficient regions.

Non-Markovian Corrections to Pretrained Weather Models

Apr. – Aug. 2026 · with Prof. Dmitry Abanin, Princeton

Leading neural weather forecasters predict from a short window of recent states, a Markovian approximation in the input representation; coarse-graining induces history dependence, motivating a correction with memory. I augmented a frozen pretrained GraphCast with a trainable correction branch — graph message passing plus Mamba layers whose latent state persists across autoregressive steps — training only the branch.

Result: at 1° resolution, normalized weighted MSE falls by 16.3% over ten-day forecasts and 23.7% at day ten; state-reset ablations show the temporal memory contributes beyond the added capacity.

  • state-space models
  • Mamba
  • graph neural networks
  • scientific ML

Cooling Algorithms for Ground-State Preparation

Mar. 2025 – present · with Prof. Biao Lian and Prof. Dmitry Abanin, Princeton

Cooling-protocol design cast as an optimal control problem trading fidelity against mixing time. Shaping the system–ancilla coupling via van Hove singularities reaches the Pareto frontier and beats prior protocols on gapless free-fermion, topological, and interacting systems. An adaptive particle-number extension is a partially observed sequential decision problem: each cycle's measurement sets the next chemical-potential correction.

  • optimal control
  • partially observed decision making
  • quantum many-body
