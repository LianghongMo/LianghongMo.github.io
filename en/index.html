About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in physics at Princeton University, working at the interface of reinforcement learning, generative models, and many-body physics. I study how agents can learn the structure they need — representations, goals, and credit assignment — directly from experience in long-horizon problems with partial observation and competing objectives, and how reinforcement learning can be used to fine-tune large pretrained generative models without collapsing their diversity. Before turning to machine learning I worked on quantum many-body dynamics: driven conformal field theories, non-Hermitian systems, and entanglement structure.


Research Interests

  • Reinforcement learning: goal-conditioned and self-supervised RL, representation learning for control, long-horizon decision making
  • RL fine-tuning of generative models: policy optimization, credit assignment, reward hacking and diversity collapse; LLM post-training
  • Sequence and state-space models for physical dynamics
  • Quantum many-body dynamics and quantum information

Current Research

  • Scaling contrastive reinforcement learning (with Prof. Benjamin Eysenbach and Caleb Cheuk, Princeton, 2026–present). Goal-conditioned RL in which the critic is learned as a similarity score between representations of state–action pairs and future goals, with no hand-designed reward.
  • Diversity-aware step weighting for RL fine-tuning of diffusion models (2026–present; code). Reward–diversity collapse is the fixed point of the reward objective, so the Pareto frontier is the right target. DASW replaces DDPO's uniform per-step weight with a budget-conserving weight toward the denoising steps where generations still branch, improving both reward and diversity over DDPO with less reward hacking.
  • Non-Markovian corrections to pretrained weather models (with Prof. Dmitry Abanin, 2026). A trainable correction branch of graph message passing and Mamba layers, with latent state persisting across autoregressive steps, on top of a frozen GraphCast; cuts ten-day forecast error by 16% at 1° resolution.
  • Cooling algorithms for ground-state preparation (with Prof. Biao Lian and Prof. Dmitry Abanin, 2025–present). Cooling-protocol design as an optimal control problem trading fidelity against mixing time, plus adaptive particle-number control as a partially observed sequential decision problem.
